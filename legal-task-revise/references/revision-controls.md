# Revision controls

## Independent analysis with proportionate scope

Reviews are one source of issues. Independently assess the work product against
the assignment, record, and applicable drafting mode, including the prose and
source-verification guidance in [legal writing](legal-writing.md). Look for
missed defects in reasoning, factual application, practical advice, precision,
structure, voice, and unnecessary filler. Do not wait for a reviewer to identify
an obvious quality problem or assume a favorable review makes the documents sound.

For a large pack, map the files and concentrate substantive inspection on the
reference answer, essential matter documents, identified problem areas, and
dependencies of proposed changes. Sample lower-priority document groups; broaden
that inspection when recurring defects or material inconsistencies emerge. A
sampled style problem justifies checking similar passages, not automatically
rewriting every file in the group. Do not replace necessary source reading with
sampling when an actual amendment depends on that source.

Rank findings by their effect on legal correctness and practical use, then by
the value of the drafting improvement. Preserve usable content and use local
edits where they suffice. For a memorandum, choose a clean redraft when fixing
the structure and voice that way is faster than editing passage by passage.
Check the redraft against the assignment and the material facts, authority,
reasoning, counterarguments, conclusions and qualifications in the record.
A large file count or a desire for uniformity is insufficient reason for a
wholesale revision. Once material defects and affected dependencies are
repaired, finish verification.

Record independent findings separately from reviewer feedback in the issue
ledger. In the overview, identify meaningful independent improvements and the
actual inspection coverage; a file left uninspected is not a file found sound.

## Decide whether a review identifies a defect

For each actionable point, find the exact file, passage, or criterion. Check
whether the review used the right document version, date, jurisdiction, task
scope, audience, and required product. Separate an actual omission from a fact
already supplied elsewhere, an issue the lawyer is meant to analyze, and a
reviewer's preferred style. Repetition is not corroboration.

Accept a correction when the record and assignment support it. Modify a sound
concern whose suggested solution overreaches. Reject a concern contradicted by
the sources or based only on formulaic criticism. Mark it unresolved where the
evidence cannot decide it. Record the reason, not a dismissive label such as
“slop.” No fixed number of accepted review points is required.

Examples of decisions:

- A reviewer says no governing date was provided, but the assignment supplies
  one: reject that premise, and check whether the reference actually honors it.
- A reviewer asks for a definitive outcome where the assignment seeks risk
  advice: preserve calibrated advice instead of inventing certainty.
- A reviewer objects to a large file set without identifying irrelevant items:
  assess each file's function; do not delete volume merely to satisfy the review.
- Two reviewers disagree about a deadline: resolve the underlying dates,
  governing rule, and triggering event. Do not pick the latest review by default.

## Protect the record while improving realism

Keep a material-fact map before editing. Lock names, capacities, dates, amounts,
quoted words, defined terms, key events, admissions, denials, and knowledge
boundaries unless a supported correction specifically requires a change. A
spelling correction to a name still requires checking all dependent references.

Distinguish a packaging mistake from a purposeful evidentiary inconsistency.
An employee's mistaken date, an adverse admission, a contested account, or a
defective clause may be the point of the exercise. Preserve that evidentiary
function. Do not repair the client's conduct or the opposing party's argument
by rewriting source evidence to make the reference easier to defend.

For synthetic emails and notes, remove redundant exposition only after ensuring
the unique fact remains available and its attribution and evidentiary strength
are preserved. A fact appearing in a lawyer's summary is not automatically an
adequate replacement for its original contemporaneous source. Make voices
natural through role, purpose, vocabulary, and context; do not manufacture slang,
errors, new interactions, or unsupported background.

Treat authentic judgments, filed documents, executed contracts, witness
statements, quotations, and signed evidence as source records. Do not rewrite
their substantive text to improve style. If a correction to an actual source
record is required, prepare a separate proposed correction and explain the
issue. For an editable synthetic draft, make only the authorized repair.

## Keep the exercise coherent

For a supported substantive change, trace consequences through:

assignment → matter evidence → reference analysis/output → scoring.

Do not introduce answer cues into the prompt or matter pack, add facts only
because the rubric expects them, or use the reference as proof of a disputed
fact. Keep the task's intended difficulty and legitimate alternative analyses.
Correct implausible briefing or deadlines only on a supported basis; do not
invent partner instructions, staffing, settlement authority, or missing evidence.

## Verify according to what changed

Every changed factual proposition needs a stable source location. Every changed
legal proposition needs applicable support or an explicit unresolved status.
Recheck the final reference's consequential unchanged propositions as well;
polishing them must not imply they were verified when they were not.

Verify facts, operative language, quotations, calculations, chronology, and
cross-references after prose editing. Do not use a token-preservation check as
proof of semantic equivalence: changing “may” to “must” or relocating an exception
can alter legal effect while preserving names and numbers.

The final check covers all supplied reviews, independent findings, every file's
inspection status and disposition, all
changed criteria and their dependencies, and the alignment of the actual final
files. Record unreadable or unverified portions accurately. If an unresolved
issue prevents a reliable final document, identify that affected deliverable as
incomplete and continue independent corrections; do not disguise the gap with
unsupported drafting.
